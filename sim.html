What you are looking at

no real money has been spent — this is a scorecard, not a wallet

We watch 58 crypto wallets. 30 of them have a good track record. Every time one of those 30 buys a coin, we pretend to buy $100 of the same coin at the same moment, and we write it down. When that wallet sells out, we pretend to sell too. Nothing is bought. Nothing is risked. The point is to find out — before any real money is involved — whether following these people would actually have made money.

Why our price is worse than theirs
Their own purchase pushes the price up before we can react. We can never buy at the price the person we are copying got. This is measured from the size of their order against the size of the pool.
Why our own order costs us too
These coins trade against a pot of money. Buying takes coins out and pushes the price up against us. A $100 order into a small pot moves the price far more than the same order into a big one.
The house cut
Every trade pays a fee to the pool. We measured the real fee coin by coin — it ranges from 0.3% to over 5%, not the flat rate the first version assumed.
You pay all of it twice
Getting out costs the same as getting in. A round trip on a typical coin here costs several percent before the price has moved at all — that is the toll you must beat.
